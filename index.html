Section 02 · Diagnosis

Guests are coming less often and spending less on drinks.

This section reads Ojos's own systems to explain where the sales and the profit went: Mirus net sales by week, daypart, category and check to 15 November 2026, and the R365 ledger to period 10 of FY2026, with period 11 as booked but not closed. Margin comparisons with 2025 stay on the site-level model, which ties to the ledger. Guest and check counts compare only before the Toast switch, so they use weeks 1 to 19.

Adjusted EBITDA fell from $27.2M in FY2024 to $8.5M over the last twelve months, mostly on cost rates.

Management's own monthly packs report adjusted EBITDA of $27.2M in FY2024, $20.8M in FY2025 and $8.48M over the twelve months to period 10 of FY2026, on gross sales of $167.2M, $164.6M and $158.0M. Store profit fell from $37.4M (22.4% of sales) to $20.05M (12.7%).

What holds. The packs tie: fiscal-year sales less comps are within 0.2% of the audited sales, store profit over the twelve months ($20,054,959) matches CreditSwan's R365 figure to about $5K.

What to watch. Sales fell only about 6%. Most of the fall came from cost rates: labor rose from 22.6% to 26.7% of sales, occupancy and other store costs from 31.4% to 34.7%, and comps from 1.6% to 3.0%. History was also restated between packs after the finance team changed in early 2026, so the figures here use the latest pack for each period (Appendix, Reporting consistency).

The fall started in FY2025, not with the World Cup. Store margin was 22.4% in FY2024 and 19.1% in FY2025. In periods 8 to 10, adjusted EBITDA was $5.9M in 2024, $4.5M in 2025 and $0.2M in 2026. Over periods 1 to 10, FY2026 earned $6.8M against $19.0M a year earlier.

Cost rates, not lost sales, explain most of the fall. Valued at FY2024's store margin, the lower sales cost about $2.1M. Labor running 4.1 points higher as a share of sales cost about $6.6M, occupancy and other store costs about $5.2M, comps about $2.3M and cost of goods about $1.2M; overhead net of add-backs rose $1.4M.

Stores served about an eighth fewer guest checks early this year.

At the 32 stores open in both years, Mirus recorded about 1,870 guest checks a store-week in weeks 1 to 19 of FY2026, against about 2,150 a year earlier: 12.9% fewer. Those weeks ran on the old point-of-sale system in both years. From 11 May to 10 August each store moved to Toast, which counts checks and guests differently, so later weeks are not compared.

What holds. Net sales do compare across the switch and show the same shape: the same 32 stores were 14.4% below last year over weeks 1 to 46 of FY2026.

What to watch. A comparable guest count after the switch needs Toast's check definition from Ojos, or loyalty visits once Paytronix access arrives.

Caution · checks after the Toast switch

Toast went live store by store from 11 May to 10 August 2026 and counts checks and guests differently from the old system. Sales compare across the switch; checks, guests and spend per check do not, so this section uses weeks 1 to 19.

Sources: Mirus daily sales file, FY2025 and FY2026 to 15 Nov 2026. Assumptions A-02 and A-03.

Guests spent less on drinks per check, while the food check rose.

In weeks 1 to 19 at the same 32 stores, net sales per check fell from $47.44 to $44.51 (−6.2%). Liquor per check fell 15.8% to $9.56 and beer 9.1% to $17.94, while food, including online orders, rose 7.8% to $15.88 after menu price increases.

What holds. The food check is rising, so the guests who still come order food at higher prices.

What to watch. Drinks carry a lower cost of goods than food, so a guest who drinks less is worth much less profit. The October drink price rise is too recent to show in these weeks.

Sources: Mirus daily sales and major-category files. Assumption A-04.

Alcohol has shrunk as a share of sales, which lowers margin.

At the same 32 stores, weeks 1 to 46 of FY2026 against the same weeks of FY2025 on Mirus net, liquor sales fell 22.0% and beer 15.6%, against 5.5% for food including online orders. Total sales fell 14.4%, from $142.4M to $121.9M. Beer and liquor carry about four fifths of the fall, and liquor's share of sales fell from 22.9% to 20.9%.

What holds. Food has held up best: with online orders it is now about 35% of sales at these stores.

What to watch. Drinks cost less to serve than food, so the shift raises the cost-of-goods rate (next section but one).

Sources: Mirus major-category and product-mix files to 15 Nov 2026. Assumption A-02.

Late night has fallen fastest, by more than a fifth in recent weeks.

Mirus splits sales by daypart. At the same 32 stores, weeks 1 to 46 of FY2026, late night fell 29.3% and carries 35% of the fall in sales, against dinner −14.9%, happy hour −10.5% and lunch −6.9%. In the six weeks to 15 November, late night was 34.0% below last year while lunch was 2.6% above. Part of the late-night fall is the Toast switch: in the week each store moved to Toast, about 2.3 points of its sales moved out of late night and about 3.2 points into happy hour, against the same weeks at stores not yet switched. Allowing for it, late night fell about 25% over weeks 1 to 46 (about 30% of the fall in sales) and about 21% in the six weeks, and is still the weakest daypart.

What holds. Lunch and happy hour have come back close to last year, with lunch above it in periods 11 and 12 so far.

What to watch. Management's slide puts period 11 lunch at +16.7% and late night at about −39%; on Mirus at the same 32 stores they were +2.5% and −29.0%. The daypart hours each uses need confirming with Ojos.

Sources: Mirus daypart file to 15 Nov 2026; management's update, 9 Nov 2026, slide 3. Assumption A-07.

The World Cup lifted sales briefly, and the weeks after fell back.

At the same 32 stores, the six World Cup weeks (24 to 29 of FY2026) were 3.8% above last year on Mirus net. Across all stores, period 7 was the best of the year at $13.9M, and periods 8 and 9 fell to about $10.6M each, about 13% below 2025. Weeks 20 to 46 without the World Cup weeks were 15.9% below last year. Management points to high gas prices and back-to-school costs.

What holds. Sales have improved since, to $11.2M in period 10 and $11.3M in period 11.

What to watch. There is no World Cup in 2027, so period 7 will be a hard comparison next year.

Sources: Mirus daily sales file; management's update, 9 Nov 2026, slide 3. Assumption A-01.

Fight nights lost most of their lift when UFC left pay-per-view.

Until January 2026 the stores showed numbered UFC cards on pay-per-view and charged a cover. On Mirus, a card Saturday sold about a quarter more than the neighbouring Saturdays in the pay-per-view era; in 2026, with UFC on a streaming subscription, the lift is about 9%, or about $1.6K a store. Covers and tickets, which Mirus books as retail, fell from $2.02M in FY2024 and $1.69M in FY2025 to $0.18M in FY2026 to 15 November. Together that is a gap of about $2.6M of sales a year (Section 05).

What holds. Big cards still lift sales: the weeks of UFC 331, UFC 333 and the Canelo Álvarez fight were the three best of the autumn (Exhibit 2.27). R365 also books $77K and $106K of covers in periods 8 and 9 of 2026, which the site-level model leaves out of sales.

What to watch. Which stores still charge a cover, and on which cards, needs confirming with Ojos; management is moving the brand away from cover charges.

Sources: Mirus daily, category and product-mix files; R365 sales accounts; events calendar, August to December 2026. Assumption A-15.

Store margin fell as labor and promotions stayed up while sales dropped.

On the site-level model, which ties to the R365 ledger, store margin fell from about 21% of sales in the first ten periods of 2025 to about 13% in 2026. Labor took the largest share, about 3.5 points, because labor cost per store stayed flat while sales per store fell about 13%. Promotions and comps nearly doubled as a share of sales, and cost of goods and rent each took more than a point. The latest quarter is weaker still: in periods 8 to 10, store profit was $2.75M against $6.93M a year earlier, with cost of goods at 23.8% of sales (21.3%), labor at 29.0% (23.9%) and comps at 3.0% (2.1%).

What holds. Facility, repair and utility costs fell by about $1.6M, and security and entertainment were cut.

What to watch. Crew hours have not followed sales. At the same 32 stores in the six weeks to 15 November, crew hours were 0.2% below last year on sales 9.0% lower, and sales per crew hour fell from $58.0 to $52.9 (R365 punch data, Mirus net). That is the store labor lever in Section 06.

Sources: Site-level operating model, 2 Nov 2026; R365 punch data and Mirus net sales, six weeks to 15 Nov 2026. Assumption A-05.

Cost of goods rose most in liquor, where the rate stepped up in late 2025.

In periods 8 to 10 of 2026, cost of goods was 23.8% of sales against 21.3% a year earlier, about $3.5M a year at 2026 sales (R365 cost with inventory counts, on the site-level model's sales). Mix explains 0.76 points, because drinks and covers fell faster than food. The rest is cost rates: liquor 0.92 points, food 0.63 and beer 0.13. Each of the 32 same stores rose, by 0.8 to 4.0 points.

What holds. The beer cost rate barely moved, from 21.1% to 21.5%. BOGO wings explain about 0.23 points of the rise.

What to watch. Liquor cost per liquor sales dollar rose from 11.3% to 14.2% (14.4% to 19.0% with bar items), from period 13 of 2025, before Toast and BOGO. Back at the 2025 rate it is worth about $0.8M to $1.3M a year; the cause is a question for Ojos, so it is listed as a candidate and not counted.

The liquor rate stepped up in period 13 of 2025. Cost per liquor sales dollar, with bar consumables and Red Bull, ran at 14% to 15% through FY2025, rose to 17.6% in period 13 and has been 18% to 19.5% since period 4 of 2026. Food stepped up in period 8 of 2026, when inventory counts took $231K out of stock.

What management says. On 20 November management explained the step-up. In late 2025 Ojos added a 22oz frozen mug at $1 more than the 16oz, to lift the check and the profit on each drink, and a daily specials menu with a few discounted shots, cocktails, longnecks, ballenas or buckets each day, as a value offer to slow the loss of guests seen from 2024 to 2025. A much larger pour for $1 more, and daily discounts, both raise the cost of each liquor dollar. Guest counts and liquor spend per check kept falling, so the offers have not yet visibly paid for themselves. Two actions follow: re-price the 22oz mug, and keep only the daily specials that bring guests in. The lever stays a candidate, not counted, until Mirus and Toast item sales show the mug's share and the specials' volumes.

Same-store sales improved in the autumn, led by Houston and Dallas.

The Mirus daily file runs to 15 November, six weeks past period 10. At the same 32 stores, net sales were 16.9% below last year in the six weeks to 27 September and 9.0% below in the six weeks to 15 November; over weeks 1 to 46 of FY2026 they were 14.4% below (13.9% on Ojos's own same-store rule). The twelve Houston and Dallas stores were 0.7% below last year over the latest six weeks, while the other twenty were 14.6% below. Net sales fall about two points more than the gross figures in the weekly reports, because BOGO and comps sit between gross and net.

What holds. Houston and Dallas earned about two thirds of store profit over the twelve months to period 10, and almost all of it in periods 8 to 10 of 2026 ($2.06M of $2.27M), so their recovery carries the most weight.

What to watch. Part of the gain is an easier comparison, since sales were already falling a year ago, and guest counts do not compare across the Toast switch.

The three best weeks each carried a major fight. The weeks with UFC 331 (to 20 September, −4.7%), UFC 333 (to 25 October, −4.6%) and the Canelo Álvarez fight (to 1 November, −6.9%) were the three best of the fourteen. The UFC 332 week (to 4 October) was seventh. Last year's calendar matters too, so this is a pattern to test card by card (Section 05).

California is the only region still getting worse. Its three comparable stores were 17.8% below last year in the six weeks to 27 September and 25.4% below in the six weeks to 15 November. Chula Vista was 36.6% below.

Guests rate Ojos Locos close to its rivals, but it has the most detractors and the weakest service scores.

Ojos commissioned a customer study from Beckett Square during the refinancing, with a final readout on 1 September 2026. It surveyed 1,000 sports-bar and casual-dining guests, 251 of them recent Ojos Locos guests, and interviewed five Ojos guests. Ojos Locos has a net promoter score of 48, against 44 to 54 for six rivals, and the highest share of detractors, 12%. It scores best of the seven on drinks quality and third on sports viewing, and lowest on menu variety, overall atmosphere, speed of service and comfort and safety.

What holds. The survey agrees with the system data on where the business is made: 63% of Ojos guests come after dinner or late at night, the highest of the seven brands, and late night is the daypart that has fallen most (−29.3% at the same 32 stores, weeks 1 to 46 of FY2026). Guests liked the 2026 food and drink offers and the daily and late-night specials (72% and 70% positive).

What to watch. The sample leans towards Ojos guests and is not representative of the market, and all seven scores sit within ten points of each other. The survey was taken before the October drink price rise and the two late-night offers. Nevada's score rests on nine guests.

What it means for the plan. Beckett Square judges that price rises are near their ceiling and that value offers and a loyalty programme will do more, and Ojos guests weigh drink prices more than the wider sample does. So the October drink price rise needs reading in drinks sold per check, not only in the check. Speed of service is Ojos's weakest score, so the crew-hours lever should start late at night, go store by store and watch service times. The lighter door security saves money but has a cost in visits: a quarter of guests disliked it and about a third of those say they come less, and crowd control is one of the study's main problems, so the further $0.6M of security savings management expects in 2027 should be decided store by store with incident data. Guests were split on fewer DJ nights, which supports testing them against held-out stores rather than restoring them everywhere. The loyalty recommendation fits the review of Paytronix once access is in place.

The guest and enforcement. Beckett Square finds the Ojos guest particularly exposed to immigration enforcement: guests cut back for themselves and for friends and family, and the police presence expected around a late-night bar, mostly for drink-driving, keeps some away. It suggests rideshare partnerships and talks with local police. This is the first evidence from Ojos's own guests beside the outside sources in The guest (Exhibits 3.04 and 3.21); it does not size the effect on sales.

Guests who stop coming. Among guests who know Ojos Locos but have never been, 63% say it is too far and 18% that it is not family-friendly; among those who have not been for a year, 46% and 25%. Guests age out as they settle down or have children, so the study puts more weight on winning new guests.

By state. Net promoter score is 60 in California (42 guests), 53 in Arizona (59), 50 in Texas (131) and in New Mexico (10), and 11 in Nevada, on nine guests too few to read.
